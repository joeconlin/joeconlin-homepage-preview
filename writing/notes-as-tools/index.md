# Notes are tools for thinking

By Joe Conlin · 17 October 2026

Canonical HTML: https://joeconlin.github.io/joeconlin-homepage-preview/writing/notes-as-tools/

> Sample copy for review. This demonstration text is not a statement of Joe’s views.

![An original geometric illustration of a folded sheet with a small blue annotation.](https://joeconlin.github.io/joeconlin-homepage-preview/images/paper-fold.svg)

Original illustration made for this prototype.

Notes are often treated as storage: a place to put something so it will not be forgotten. That is useful, but incomplete. A note can also be a tool for changing what you notice.

When I write an idea in my own words, I find the missing step. When I return to it later, I can see what has shifted. The page is not a vault; it is a workbench.

![An original abstract drawing of a page margin, ruled lines, and a blue mark connecting two ideas](https://joeconlin.github.io/joeconlin-homepage-preview/images/margin-study.svg)

A margin is a useful place for a thought to meet another one. Original illustration made for this prototype.

I am trying a simple habit: keep a short record of questions, observations, and decisions; then revisit them when the same problem returns. The value is not in collecting more. It is in making a past thought available to a present one.

![Wide original line illustration of three paths converging at a marked point](https://joeconlin.github.io/joeconlin-homepage-preview/images/field-lines.svg)

Three paths, one question. Wide illustration for testing the article’s full measure.

This is a practice, not a system I have perfected. I am curious what changes when notes are written to be used, rather than merely kept.
